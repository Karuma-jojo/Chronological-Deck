# T25: M.Stat first, with a deliberate companion exam

Reviewed 11 September 2026. This is an entrance study route inside Chrono-Deck, alongside T22 quantitative research and T23 scientific computing. It is a scope map, study launcher and evidence log—not a completed textbook, solved-paper collection or admission prediction.

## Why a separate terminal

T22 covers a much broader research destination. Requiring its 596 atomic ARCs before practising entrance questions would introduce unnecessary prerequisites. Conversely, clearing an entrance exercise should not certify mastery of an entire research module.

T25 therefore uses new stable world IDs **ARC801–ARC904**, while linking to relevant T22 atomic ARCs as supporting study. It adds no prerequisites to existing routes, does not infer completion from previous broad-module checkmarks, and does not repurpose ARC735–ARC738. T24 is not allocated by this change.

The M.Stat core has **46 units**. These are topic contracts of unequal breadth, not 46 identically sized lessons. A unit can need several short investigations and many ordinary exercises. The optional catalogue adds 58 units across other exams; they are not part of the M.Stat completion target unless the selected extension needs them.

| M.Stat + GATE DA | 61 | Choose for a computing destination. Adds Python, algorithms, databases, ML and AI. |
| M.Stat + ISI MSQE | 54 | Choose when economics is an actual interest. Micro and macro are substantial additional subjects. |
| M.Stat + GATE MA | 63 | Specialist alternative requiring advanced analysis, algebra, topology, ODE/PDE and numerical methods. |
| M.Stat + GATE CS | 62 | Specialist alternative requiring C, algorithms, systems, automata, compilers and networks. |

Counts measure catalogue organisation, not workload or difficulty. A broad optional module can be much larger than a core unit. One extension is displayed at a time; changing it preserves all concept and practice records.

For this goal, begin with **M.Stat**, adding the small CMI extension when the common foundations are stable. Choose ST or DA only after checking which exact postgraduate programmes accept that paper and your eventual degree. GATE permits at most two papers in 2027; ST + DA is currently allowed, as are the other pairs among the four papers considered here. Recheck the permitted combinations in the application year. GATE examination eligibility does not establish eligibility for an individual IISc, IIT, ISI or CMI programme. Degree nomenclature, transcript requirements and programme-specific admissions need their own verification.

The map is based on the whole supplied M.Stat syllabus, not only the 2026 sample questions. Its 15 scope groupings cover elementary mathematics, calculus/linear algebra, probability and distributions, sampling laws/limits, descriptive statistics, estimation, inference/regression, designs and sampling. The other exam maps cover all their official section groups, with shared prerequisites included. Individual unit bullets are concise checklists; consult the full official source for finer subtopics and exact wording.

An official syllabus is a scope reference, not a proof authority. Verify each mathematical derivation against a suitable textbook/course or an independent derivation when you actually study it. Related T22 links can cover only part of an entrance unit; missing concepts remain explicit work, particularly order statistics, sufficiency, method of moments, Latin squares and survey-sampling details.

## How to use the page

1. Open **T25 · ISI entrance preparation** in the header, or choose T25 in the terminal selector.
2. Leave the plan at **M.Stat core only** initially. Use the full coverage table to check what each unit is responsible for.
3. Select a unit from the study dropdown or graph. Read its target and checklist. Existing T22 links open the parent module; select the named atomic ARC there for depth.
4. Choose **Spire investigation**, **Learn prerequisites**, **Written practice**, **Delayed review**, or **Small project**. Open the prompt, copy it, and paste it into a study chat with your Spire instructions and relevant source material.
5. Record written attempts with a question reference, assistance level, date, optional time/marks and a specific correction. Use the concept checkmark only when your evidence supports the whole unit target.
6. Export the practice evidence periodically. Export world/T22 progress separately with their existing controls.

Nothing launches an external model or transmits practice records when you choose a prompt. The website prepares text for your study chat. It does not silently mark a unit cleared or verify a claimed official question.

## A demanding but sustainable study loop

Use Spire for a worthwhile conceptual obstacle: why the power rule has its form, why an order-statistic density contains its combinatorial factor, or why conditioning can improve an estimator. Use ordinary teaching for notation and definitions, and ordinary practice for fluency. Do not turn every theorem, algebra convention and repeated calculation into a week-long discovery wall.

A session should have one explicit mathematical deliverable. Establish the needed definitions and allowed lemmas before sealing a wall. During a V11.3 investigation, preserve the existing control semantics: **[WALL] does not expire**; help requires the learner's explicit control. If progress stalls, end or park the session and ask whether to invoke an allowed help mode or repair a prerequisite. Never quietly switch to hints or inflate aided work into independent mastery.

A practical sequence is: a short prerequisite check; one bounded discovery or worked example; a small set of written problems; then a delayed unaided reconstruction and an unfamiliar problem. Spacing, retrieval, explanatory questions and interleaving worked examples with problem solving are supported by the [IES study-practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1). This does not establish an optimal percentage allocation or guarantee exam results.

Begin topical official questions as soon as the relevant material is available. Do not wait until the final synthesis unit. Distinguish an official sample paper from an administered paper. If the actual paper is unavailable, use honestly labelled original exam-style problems; never invent a year or question number. Reserve some unseen papers for later timed assessment, while using other papers for learning.

The graph's prerequisites describe preparation for a whole unit. They are planning guides, not a ban on exploring a simpler part. For example, finite expected payoff in the Probability Casino needs weighted sums; it does not need calculus. The full random-variable/MGF unit later needs differentiation and integration. A brief diagnostic can justify moving past a prerequisite you already know; don't repeat an entire earlier course because a checkbox is empty.

Near an application, written papers, correction and weak-area repair should take precedence over new project features. Difficulty should come from mathematical reasoning and unfamiliar transfer, not arbitrary time pressure, endless repetition or withholding basic notation.

## Projects and coding

| Mathematical work | Small project | What the learner owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Events, counting, conditioning, finite expectation | Probability Casino | Outcome model, exact counts, fair price, counterexample and a small simulation function. |
| Distribution transformations and order statistics | Random-Variable Laboratory | Support, analytic law, assumptions and numerical agreement checks. |
| Estimation, confidence intervals and power | Statistical Detective Agency | Likelihood, estimator/test derivation, coverage or power prediction. |
| Regression and linear algebra | Matrix Worlds / Regression Observatory | Projection reasoning, normal equations, assumptions and residual checks. |
| CRD, RBD, Latin squares, factorials | Paper-Helicopter Research Institute | Randomisation, effects, interactions, sums of squares and degrees of freedom. |
| SRS and stratification | Census of an Alien Planet | Estimator, weights, design variance, allocation and finite-population limits. |
| Optional economics | Market Town / Central Bank Laboratory | Model assumptions, equilibrium equations and comparative statics. |

AI can write most interface, graphics and repetitive plumbing. The learner should own the model, derive or explain the mathematics, write or modify a small mathematical function, and explain its tests. Syntax familiarity grows through these concrete tasks. Neither a large AI-generated application nor competitive-programming rank is a substitute for M.Stat written mathematics; DA and CS do require the computing topics in their actual syllabi. No prediction about future hiring preferences is built into this route.

- T25 concept checkmarks use existing world progress. Existing T22 atomic progress and archives retain their current meaning.

## Audit findings and verified fixes

1. **Entrance scope and research scope were not separated.** T25 now maps the whole official M.Stat syllabus, including elementary mathematics, designs and sampling, with optional exam branches. T22's 58-module research contract is preserved.
2. **T23 stage six was not supported by the graph renderer.** Stage bounds, column count and viewBox width now follow terminal stage names; long headings wrap. Five-stage routes retain five columns.
3. **T22 power-rule prerequisites left function arithmetic implicit.** A04 now explicitly supplies pointwise function addition/scalar multiplication and finite limit laws before asking for linearity. The positive-integer proof distinguishes fixed x/n, n=1 and constants. No historical learner transcript was rewritten.
4. **Probability-axiom wording conflated assumptions with derived rules.** M17 now says to state the axioms and derive the resulting rules, rather than derive the axioms.
5. **README duplicated obsolete 630-node content and referred to a nonexistent setup filename.** It now documents the current structure and points to existing setup/archive interfaces. Visible world counts update from the loaded registry.

Validation covers unique/stable IDs, prerequisite closure and order for all seven plans, source-map resolution, T22 atomic links, unchanged legacy route contracts, corrupt/invalid evidence handling, merge conflicts, negative marking, copied-prompt conditions, actual entrypoint/module reachability and the six-stage renderer. Existing world, T22, Markdown and archive-contract gates also run.

This is a curriculum and frontend integration audit. It is not a new security audit of the existing cloud backend, an independent proof audit of all 596 T22 ARCs, a review of every historical transcript, or a visual browser test. This change is delivered on a review branch; the public Pages site changes only after the branch is merged and the normal Pages publication completes.
