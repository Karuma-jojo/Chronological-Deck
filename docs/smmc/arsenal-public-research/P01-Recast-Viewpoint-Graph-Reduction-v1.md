## Discriminating mathematics

### M1. A useful recasting can be information-losing

Consider a checkerboard-colored polyomino $S$. Every domino covers one black and one white cell, so

$$
S\text{ tileable}\ \Longrightarrow\ b(S)=w(S).
$$

The reverse implication is false, even for connected shapes. Our original six-cell example is

$$
S=\{(0,0),(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,0),(2,0),(1,1)\}.
$$

It has three cells of each color. The two cells $(-1,0)$ and $(0,-1)$ each have the same sole available neighbor $(0,0)$. A tiling would have to use that neighbor twice, which is impossible. By contrast, a 2-by-3 rectangle has the same color counts and admits tilings. The checkerboard-count projection preserves a necessary condition but loses information relevant to construction.

This falsifies the proposed rule “every legitimate recasting must be a bijective encoding of all solutions.” An obstruction recast needs a valid implication in the direction used. It does **not** falsify equivalence of the two Zeitz headings: a broad interpretation of viewpoint can also include selecting an invariant.

### M2. Viewpoint change and the shortcut it enables have different hypotheses

For an original constant-drift model, let shore position be $x$, drift speed $c$, water-relative swimming speed $u>c>0$, and initial upstream duration $\tau>0$. Introduce the drifting coordinate

$$
y=x-ct.
$$

The drifting object has $y=0$; the swimmer has velocities $-u$ and $+u$ before and after turning. At turn time its coordinate is $-u\tau$; it therefore needs another $\tau$ to return. This establishes the equal-duration shortcut under explicit speed assumptions. Here $u$ is water-relative; no informal speed description is silently substituted for it.

If the water-relative speeds instead are $u_1$ outward and $u_2$ returning, the same coordinate change is valid, while the return duration becomes

$$
t_{\rm return}=\frac{u_1\tau}{u_2}.
$$

For $u_1=2,u_2=4,\tau=1$, it is $1/2$, not 1. Thus choosing the frame and proving the simplifying symmetry are distinct mathematical obligations. Zeitz's radial-focus cross-reference also defeats our attempted boundary “viewpoint is always a lossless uniform translation.” The source itself uses a different focus and supplementary symmetry.

This is a successful scope test for a particular deployment and a **failed** attempt to separate the two general headings. It should not be dressed up as a counterexample to their possible synonymy.

### M3. A concrete reduction requires more than a new description

Our background algebraic reconstruction uses the cofactor identity for a unit row or unit column. If its unique nonzero entry is at $(i,j)$, then

$$
\det B=(-1)^{i+j}\det B_{\widehat{i},\widehat{j}},
$$

using consistent row/column indices. The reduction preserves magnitude, and exact sign can be tracked. A zero row/column certifies zero instead. Each reduction decreases both dimensions. If a nonempty binary square terminal matrix has every row sum 2 and no column sum below 2, its total number of ones is $2n$, so every column sum is exactly 2 as well. The corresponding loopless multigraph is 2-regular. These are explicit hypotheses for the mathematical reconstruction, not a grant of broader historical evidence to the candidate.

Three independent original boundary checks show what the graph representation must retain:

- **Sign:** the matrix $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant 2. Swapping its first two rows gives determinant $-2$, while the edge multiset is unchanged. Forgetting row order is compatible with magnitude, not with recovering signed determinant without extra data.
- **Multiplicity:** $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ gives two parallel edges, not a single edge; its determinant is zero. Collapsing it to a simple graph erases a row and destroys the square incidence correspondence.
- **Weights:** $\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$ has determinant 2, whereas replacing its nonzero entries with ones gives determinant 1. Extending a binary rule by erasing coefficients fails. A weighted variant might exist with additional bookkeeping; it is not established by this occurrence.

These tests show why “reinterpret as a graph” and “execute a determinant-preserving graph-core reduction” have different operation contracts. They do not prove that the entire official workflow is properly contained in the open-ended domain of either Zeitz heading. Such a relation would require comparing a complete operational specification at the same level.
