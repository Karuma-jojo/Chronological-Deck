# Foundation ladder v1 — implementation and review handoff

## Scope

The companion previously offered eight neutral units and 16 tasks, alongside 88 historical records. This change adds an entry chapter for a learner who has finished M01 and M02 through S09. It contains six learning steps, 12 original exercises, four guided checks, optional hints, worked references and task-specific checking criteria. Total: 14 units and 28 tasks, plus four guided checks.

This is the first chapter, not a completed two-year course. The later proof, counting, number-theory, graph, analysis and geometry route is explicitly labelled planned. It does not grant T25 clearance, certify SMMC readiness, or modify historical problem requirements or exposure.

## Design

1. Zeros and complete solution sets: zero-product reasoning and safe rearrangement.
2. What a square tells you: lower/upper bounds and attainability.
3. Parameters: exceptional coefficients and coincident roots.
4. Composition: outer conditions, inner ranges and complete branches.
5. Mixed set A: simultaneous constraints and a counterexample construction.
6. Mixed set B: a two-case minimisation and a universal parameter condition, preferably attempted later.

The first eight tasks are explicitly supported practice. Their methods are taught nearby; they must not be reported as unseen discovery. The final four require selecting a representation without a method label beside the task. Familiar ideas are recombined; no untaught theorem is intended. These difficulty labels describe scaffolding, not empirically calibrated SMMC difficulty.

The six steps arise from four teaching needs plus an immediate and delayed mixed set. Two independent workspaces per step give contrasting applications; the count is not a required template for future chapters.

Each reference was manually derived. Solutions address completeness, parameter exceptions and equality cases. Alternative correct arguments are acceptable. This is an author check, not an independent adversarial certification or a learner trial. Examples and questions use standard elementary mathematics and were newly written here; no claim of globally novel problems is made.

## Learning evidence and storage

- Existing neutral attempt/export/import/cloud mechanisms are reused with additive IDs and the same schema version.
- A saved attempt unlocks a hint/reference. A blank guided answer cannot reveal guided feedback.
- Hint use remains assisted on later attempts. Earlier reference exposure forces a later attempt to be recorded as revealed and retains `referenceSeenBefore`.
- Counts say **attempted**, not mastered. Unit self-report still does not certify a unit or unlock a historical problem.
- Guided drafts use the existing local draft mechanism and are labelled local, unassessed work. They are not included in the assessment export.
- Help content is lazy-loaded on explicit help actions. This is an instructional reveal barrier, not a security boundary against source inspection.
- Fresh users enter the foundation chapter; existing saved positions and historical/legacy deep links remain valid.
- Historical paper/metadata exposure behaviour is preserved. In the existing implementation, opening these does not automatically record exposure; a future sealed-mock feature must address that separately.

## Verification

The authoring validator checks the combined banks and additive foundation module. The browser workflow retains the legacy checks and exercises all twelve new tasks: save, hint, reference, retained exposure, every guided surface, route navigation, reload, export/import and mobile overflow. Screenshot paths are temporary test outputs.

Browser checks are interaction evidence; they do not establish pedagogical completeness or mathematical correctness. No real learner progress was edited.
